Tis an age which has gone past since a new book has begun. We began this prior one in Vancouver, which feels both near and far ago. Winter writings definitely laid the ground for it, but the fruits began to blossom during the coming spring. Through the age past we wrote ferviously, during the discourses, the histories and acceptance. Now a new book has begun, charactized best by us becomnig a man, and now becoming me. Who is it we are really? What positions and aims would we accept from the things within them, rather than without. What advantage do we have, virtues to enlight. Where may our foundations grow.
The first of these topics is virtue, for she marbles us in such delight, yet mingles and sways in delicate mystery. Foremost is the language used to describe her. For most words reflect something definite or categorical. "Of this is those." That or it's some kind of property, a way it's being. But this latter does not have the fairest acess to properties of motion, or relation between things. And that's what virtue is. For in life we are tasked with things, neccessarily and for all things benefit. But we often overlook these demands and focus on the goal, the fruit and the end. And this belies perhaps a warranted confidence and comfortable desire. But it does not hold virtue. What she is comes down to an awareness and submision to the prescence of task, and the more or less accepting manner of amendment, whereby our attending to such task is more or less sufficient for us to be pleased, and by that focused. The ends that come out are more like buds and droppings of a continous harvest. Virtue is for life: goals are for stopping. There is the manner of love then, which compliments, or in some cases vanguards virtue, as her upper end; or comes as an effect from a more defined rear virtue --who we may consider in isolation. At any rate, this amiability to task, the appreciation of it, the merry, in the suppleness and succeeding effect, lies a cloud of delight and cheer, and in that care and awe. I once mentioend this in terms of marks, whereby things in such a state would stand out to you and beckon you forth. Perhaps it is still suited. The difference, is that via virtue, our draw is not only born from grounds bade from duty and ease, but contained to that. "It is not so that we reach a point of stability and cheer, see the stars, then fly to them." Something I'm wondering is whhy certain things draw you. And whether there is a manner of something being drawn to you. We often find ourselves light and gleeful, for so many scents excite our mind. Yet, certain things pass by, unaware of me, and not exactly presenting themselves to me as to be drawn. Two things have occured in this. One, is I realized that I may form, and at any rate supply, our own love, to anybody and anything. For the love of a woman may have no bounds; and not all open themselves to this. Nor is it entirely proper, given the lenght of such mingling. "None act in so full out ways, only to then leave. The regard though for duration keeps the doors more or less shut less a key is had or invitation granted. Otherwise all would encourage everyone to love them openly and impulsively --for it'd be expected that they just go, thereby warranting a low standard. But committment and stay is the norm, and so not everyone and everything and every moment will do." Love may still occur. Being personal is like this, being near and present. But then lies the wish to be seen and cared for. And in part I don't know why. Is it because it opens you up to love? The pleasure is not just; it's but a moment. As we walk around, we feel this grin, and a good one when we consider the thing invoking this, about others seeing me, being impressed, admiring me, wanting me, seeing me for good and great. "I feel like I'm shining." Why is this exciting. For I admitt, when I walk through a crowd I often feel nervous, like everyone is looking at me, either down, or as though I'm a mystery. That, or I'm chest up, being noticed, people saying, 'oh my'. And glory is merely this feeling, but bridled upon your propiety rather than charm. Yet, I do not actually want anyone to speak to me. I don't want a crowd, or to sign autographs; I care not for theyre time and attneiton. I just want to be thought of. And I belive it all comes down to the matter of honor and duty. Which is to say, virtuous oblige to tasks, rather than succumbed ones, where relief and ill-merry is pursued. Wishing to be noticed comes from an imbalance, or at least untuned time of that former manner. For in virtue, who once perked up her head, showing me the green in her way, held me there, and through time and content, disposed me to her conduct and wares. In growing though, you experience often times where you are under the scalpel (so to speak), but lack the footing, or at least heart, to be so fortuitous and ample, and thereby feel beckoned to relief. "I am right though, in my suffering; the path is just. People should look at me. They know where I've come from and am returning to." Such is the mind for glory. Or, "man, those people, they don't notice me, and I'm doing so well while theyre not." Such is the mind of a cynic; who is not wrong, but patronizes others because he is alone. In my experience this came from me knowing more about virtue than deed; and thus upon the richest of successes, not truly having won. And then there is that peak of becomnig a man, whereby the slips and turns of that result in a more or less squicning and or cringing stability; as though we know about virtue and her ways, stand by our rules, and are aware of our trouble; for which we concive nor invent some remedy, but stay with the trouble, breathe, endure. Moreover, do we adapt, and rise above the conduct we once thought fit: we give credence to the hurdles around me. Much of youth is defined by such hurdles being a frustration, as though 'man, haven't we won yet! I just want to get over the things I have to do so I can pursue what I want'.
Being a man, oh how it can get drowned in action. And much of what I see is in relying on our manhood, and more or less testafying it; not putting other things up on a hill, as though theyre required. It seems better to look at them as things which would be nice and supportive while you continue to grown and maintain. Then there's those things which flow naturally, such as sums, as though fruits and or makings of a firm and steady state.
